# Demo-day template — 3 minutes, YC style

This replaces the 15-slide Airbnb format as our build target. The Airbnb **arc** survives
(pain → fix → proof → size → money → why-us → ask) but compressed into a *spoken* pitch.
`airbnb-template.md` stays as the content-quality reference; this file is the form.

## The physics of a demo-day pitch

- **3 minutes ≈ 420–450 spoken words.** Every sentence is load-bearing. Write the script
  first, make slides after — slides are billboards behind a speech, not the pitch itself.
- **The audience is a ballroom, not a boardroom.** Slides must be readable from the back
  row in 2 seconds: one idea, giant text, one number. "Visually boring" is the official
  YC advice — the founder is the show.
- **The goal is a meeting, not a check.** The pitch is an advertisement. Optimize for one
  thing: an investor can repeat what you do, correctly, to their partner an hour later.
- **First sentence = what you do**, so plainly a 5th grader could repeat it. No category
  jargon, no "platform for synergies."
- **Traction always carries a time frame.** Seibel's #1 flagged mistake: a number with no
  clock on it. "36,913 downloads" is noise; "21,001 downloads in the last 30 days,
  growing 2.2× month over month, launched in January" is a pitch.
- **No live demo.** Despite the name, nobody demos. A 10-second animated screenshot
  beats a live terminal that might hang.

## The script skeleton (~175s spoken + walk-on/off buffer)

Each slide: **SAY** (the script, with word budget) and **SHOW** (the billboard text).

---

### 1. Who we are — 10s (~25 words)
Compresses Airbnb slide 1 (Cover).
- SHOW: logo + one-liner, nothing else.
- SAY: "Hi, I'm ___. Smithers is ___." One sentence, plain words, names the old thing it replaces.
- SCRIPT: `________`

### 2. Problem — 20s (~50 words)
Compresses Airbnb slide 2.
- SHOW: the pain in ≤6 words, or one damning stat.
- SAY: the pain the room already believes, one concrete story beat, no setup.
- SCRIPT: `________`

### 3. Solution — 25s (~60 words)
Compresses Airbnb slides 3 + 7 (Solution + Product).
- SHOW: one product visual (the run tree / crash-resume moment).
- SAY: what literally happens when someone uses it — user's-eye view, benefits not architecture.
- SCRIPT: `________`

### 4. The magic moment — 15s (~35 words)
The demo-day stand-in for a live demo.
- SHOW: the one 10-second visual that makes the room lean in (candidate: kill the
  process mid-run → resume → nothing lost).
- SAY: narrate exactly what they're watching.
- SCRIPT: `________`

### 5. Traction — 30s (~70 words) ← the most important slide
Compresses Airbnb slides 5 + 13 (Validation + Press/Traction).
- SHOW: the growth graph, one headline number, the time frame.
- SAY: numbers + clock + trajectory ("launched January, 2.2× month over month").
  Add the strongest secondary proof (contributors, ecosystem, named-company conversations).
- SCRIPT: `________`

### 6. Why we win — 20s (~50 words)
Compresses Airbnb slides 10 + 11 (Competition + Advantages).
- SHOW: the insight in one line.
- SAY: the earned secret — the thing we know that incumbents can't act on, and why the
  giant shipping a look-alike validates rather than kills us.
- SCRIPT: `________`

### 7. Market — 15s (~40 words)
Compresses Airbnb slide 6.
- SHOW: one big number + its label.
- SAY: who pays, how many of them, why now.
- SCRIPT: `________`

### 8. Business model — 10s (~25 words)
Compresses Airbnb slide 8.
- SHOW: the price / the take, one line.
- SAY: how money flows, napkin-simple.
- SCRIPT: `________`

### 9. Team — 15s (~35 words)
Compresses Airbnb slide 12.
- SHOW: face(s) + one credential each.
- SAY: the single credential that makes us inevitable for this problem.
- SCRIPT: `________`

### 10. Ask + close — 15s (~35 words)
Compresses Airbnb slide 14.
- SHOW: "Raising $___" + contact.
- SAY: amount, what it buys in milestones, then re-say the one-liner verbatim from
  slide 1 — the last thing they hear is the thing they'll repeat.
- SCRIPT: `________`

---

## Build checklist

- [ ] Total script ≤ 450 words (read aloud with a timer; target 2:45, mic dies at 3:00)
- [ ] First sentence passes the repeat-test on a stranger
- [ ] Every number has a time frame
- [ ] Every slide readable in 2 seconds from 20 feet
- [ ] The one-liner appears twice: first words and last words
- [ ] Nothing on a slide that isn't said out loud within 5 seconds of it appearing
